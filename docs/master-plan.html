1. Summary

One line: Aevion lets anyone fly through a landscape, scrub through real NISAR observations, see where the surface changed, and inspect the evidence and uncertainty behind every result.

Flagship story: surface deformation from the June 2026 Venezuela earthquakes, using a NISAR interferogram pair (pre-event June 13, post-event June 25) as reported in the source plan. This is conditional on Gate 0.

Core principle: we visualize what NISAR observed, explain what we derive, show where we are uncertain, and label simulations as simulations.

What we are not building: a fire-spread predictor, a global on-demand processor, a raw SAR-to-InSAR pipeline, or a multi-mission data fusion tool.

2. Decisions

Three plans were reviewed: the original wildfire twin, TerraCascade, and the Dancing with the SARs blueprint. The blueprint is the base. Here is what we took from each.

Source planVerdictWhat we do with it
Wildfire digital twin (original) STRETCH Wildfire becomes a secondary story using GCOV backscatter, validated against FIRMS or official perimeters. Labeled “radar-observed disturbance likelihood”, not “burn detected”. Backscatter changes have many causes, so it is not the flagship.
TerraCascade CUT (mostly) It targets a different challenge (Imagine Our Connected Earth) and needs about eight datasets with mismatched grids and coverage. We keep only the ideas that strengthen validity: the badge system, a click-to-inspect value stack, and an “evidence completeness” readout.
SARs blueprint BASE NISAR is the centerpiece, earthquake deformation is the flagship, and there is honest handling of product maturity, coherence, and uncertainty. Adopted with the changes below.

Changes we made to the blueprint

  1. Added Gate 0. The whole flagship depends on data we have not confirmed exists. Verify first, and have a fallback story ready.
  2. Line-of-sight is not vertical. The blueprint extrudes terrain by LOS displacement. LOS is a projection onto the satellite’s look direction, not up/down motion. We color by LOS displacement as the primary view. Any 3D exaggeration is an optional, clearly labeled illustrative mode, with the true value always shown.
  3. Renderer decision checkpoint. Cesium is the primary choice, but we set a hard checkpoint at hour 12 of the event. If Cesium terrain plus overlay is not working by then, drop to a 2.5D view (MapLibre or deck.gl) and spend the time on explanation and uncertainty instead.
  4. Fewer, sharper features. The MVP is one finished story plus one small second story, not a platform.
  5. Evidence completeness (from TerraCascade): for any selected pixel or region, show which layers have valid data, and which are masked or missing.
  6. Static-first deployment. The demo runs entirely from prepared static files so the backend or network can fail without breaking the pitch.

  • Fallback decided (see below) in case the Venezuela pair is not usable.

5. Science rules

Layer badges

OBSERVED direct NISAR measurement or standard mission product   DERIVED computed by us with a documented method   CONTEXT DEM, optical imagery, boundaries   WHAT-IF hypothetical

Every layer shows its badge, mission and product name, acquisition dates, resolution, and a one-line method. Every derived value has a “show formula” control. No unlabeled extrapolation and no opaque scores.

Non-negotiables

  • Never mix product maturity (BETA, PROVISIONAL, VALIDATED) inside one change calculation. Pin granule IDs and CRIDs.
  • Deformation is always shown with coherence. A displacement map without its quality layer is not shipped.
  • Exaggeration is always labeled with the factor, and the true value is in every tooltip.
  • LOS means line-of-sight. We state the sign convention (toward or away from the satellite) in the legend and verify it against the published product.
  • Backscatter change is not a verdict. Use language like “radar-observed change” or “disturbance likelihood”, with alternatives listed (moisture, roughness, geometry, processing differences).
  • Do not claim NISAR reconstructs buildings, trees or cracks, or that anything is a forecast.

Known issues to surface in the UI

  • Ionospheric effects, strongest at high latitudes; residual phase can remain after correction.
  • Radio-frequency interference in some products.
  • Speckle: single-pixel differences are not change. Use spatial filtering and, where possible, a multi-date baseline.
  • Geometry: foreshortening, layover and shadow. GCOV applies radiometric terrain correction but is still not a photograph.
  • Decorrelation: vegetation, water and snow lower coherence.
  • Coverage: acquisitions are not uniform in time. Show real gaps.

Displacement from phase (for the data lead)

For an unwrapped interferogram, LOS displacement is approximately d = phase × λ / (4π), with λ about 0.24 m for NISAR L-band. Confirm the layer is already in meters, choose a stable reference area, and check the sign convention before presenting any number. Use the published GUNW or ARIA values, and cross-check ours against them.

Backscatter change (second story)

db   = 10 * log10(gcov_power)
base = median(pre_event_stack_db);  spread = MAD(pre_event_stack_db)
anomaly = (event_db - base) / spread
score = |anomaly| * valid_mask * confidence_factor

6. Product and demo

Modes

Explore (free navigation), Story (guided), Compare (pre/post slider), Evidence (provenance, uncertainty, alternatives).

Story Mode answers “what changed?”. Scientist Mode answers “what exactly did NISAR measure, and how confident are we?”.

Click-to-inspect panel

Clicking any point shows a stack: LOS displacement (true units), coherence, quality flags, acquisition dates, maturity, and which supporting layers have valid data there (evidence completeness).

Three-minute demo script

  1. Open on Earth: “Earth never stops moving. Most of that movement is invisible to us.”
  2. Fly to Venezuela. “In June 2026, earthquakes changed this landscape. NISAR passed over before and after.”
  3. Click the pre-event date, then the post-event date. The deformation field appears: “These colors are radar measurements, not an artist’s simulation.”
  4. Toggle exaggeration (if built): “We amplify centimeters so humans can see them. The numbers stay true.”
  5. Toggle coherence: “We don’t hide uncertainty. Where the measurement is unreliable, the map tells you.”
  6. Click a point: show the value stack and provenance.
  7. Zoom out to the globe with story markers: “The same engine can show many rhythms of a changing Earth.”

Do not spend the demo teaching SAR physics. Teach only what the story needs.

Accessibility

Color-blind-safe palette (not red/green only), hatching option, numeric tooltips, pause controls on every animation, story text readable without 3D motion, labeled charts.

11. Validation and testing

Scientific validation

  • Earthquake: compare against the published NASA/ARIA displacement, and GNSS or USGS information where available.
  • Flood or wetland: compare against optical water observations or authoritative flood extents.
  • Wildfire: compare against FIRMS detections, optical burn products or official perimeters.

Tests

  • Science: nodata stays nodata, units are correct, dates are correct, dB conversion is correct, maturity levels are not mixed, LOS sign matches the source product.
  • Software: tiles load, metadata schema validates, timeline state works, mobile layout works, fallback assets work offline.
  • Human: show a non-expert and ask: What changed? How do you know? How certain is it? They should answer all three from the interface alone.
